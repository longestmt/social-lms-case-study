The product

Slide 2 / 8

A social LMS focused on training and knowledge-sharing

Training

Employee training via e-learning content; managers tracked completion and progress.

Discussion

Forums let people share expertise publicly — and reward helpful answers by upvoting with “coins”.

Starting point

A prototype funded by one client’s development contracts, with two or three early adopters.

Business ambition

A SaaS product with recurring revenue in addition to custom training-content sales.

My role was to build a roadmap that retained existing clients whilst acquiring new ones.

This involved user interviews, market research, gathering requirements, design, coordinating deployments and marketing product development to new and existing customers.

Decision 01 · Prioritization

Slide 4 / 8

Admin Portal

Enable non-technical self-service. Reduce turnaround for clients with concierge admin.

My role: built the investment case with sales and engineering input, and won prioritization for it.

1 · The ask — what was being asked of us

Build a marketable Admin Portal

The existing lightweight proto-portal was designed primarily for user administration and setup tasks. This was used internally and by enthusiastic early adopters.

2 · The call — what I decided

Prioritize the feature

New customers won’t consider the product without a fully featured admin portal. It will benefit existing customers as well.

3 · The cost — what it gave up

Pushing out funded features

Tomcast’s funded features needed to be moved back while the portal was built.

I acknowledged this impact but made the importance of the feature clear and assured the client that this would improve both the speed and quality of administration on their instance.

4 · What happened

Excitement from new and existing clients

Buyers responded better in demos, and customers began administering themselves. We were eventually able to hire a customer success rep to manage instances.

Decision 02 · Scope

Slide 5 / 8

Learning Paths

Solve the common case completely instead of the general case partially.

My role: facilitated customer interviews that surfaced the real need — serialized learning. I designed the UX implementation and partnered with engineering for a quick delivery.

1 · The ask — what prospective clients requested

Conditional branching

Prospective clients asked for rule-driven course branching — a complex engine with a hard-to-build admin interface.

2 · The call — what I decided

Prerequisites

Finish one step to unlock the next. It covered the shape of nearly every training program we heard about.

3 · The cost — what it gave up

Descoped conditional logic

Requests for true branching logic remained open for some customers.

Accepted knowingly: the narrower solution shipped in days, and overcame this objection for most customers.

4 · What happened

Shipped in days, demos improved

Positive demos, fewer sales escalations, and new conversations about training content.

Signals were qualitative — which is why the speed of learning mattered as much as the feature.

Decision 03 · Funding tradeoffs

Slide 6 / 8

Rewards

Leverage our customer’s investment and innovation for the betterment of our product.

My role: developed the business rules for the feature, co-designed the UX and established the vision for differentiation.

1 · The offer — client money, attached to a feature

Funded gamification

Tomcast signed an SOW to fund “Rewards” — a feature designed to increase user engagement by allowing top users to redeem their coins for real-world prizes.

2 · The call — take the money, shape the feature

Build Rewards (for everyone)

Coins awarded for helpful forum posts become redeemable for real rewards — reusable by any customer, not just Tomcast.

3 · The cost — what it deferred

Core work pushed out

Course-standard (SCORM) updates, infrastructure, new features and iterative improvements all waited.

Justified because the funding itself kept the team alive to do that work later.

4 · What happened

Tomcast satisfied. Refined customer targeting.

We discovered that sales-oriented teams were an especially good fit for this feature set. This fun and unique engagement mechanic gave us an edge in a stuffy LMS market.

What changed · approximately 12–18 months

Slide 7 / 8

Subscriptions started carrying the team

Before

One client’s development contracts

A prototype with two or three early adopters.

After

Recurring LMS subscriptions

Revenue from Tomcast and from new customers acquired along the way.

Recurring subscription revenue covered the product team’s costs — less dependence on Tomcast’s feature SOWs created predictable funding and allowed the team to focus on achieving product-market fit.

A retrospective account. These decisions contributed alongside the wider team’s work — sales closed contracts, engineering built and ran the platform, and leadership negotiated the funding.

What I took from it

Slide 8 / 8

Three decisions, three durable rules

01

Prioritize impact

The Admin Portal won on value. It was beneficial to both concierge and self-administering customers. This feature was a great example of slowing down to go faster.

02

Scope for the actual problem

There is no substitute for talking to the customer. Most training shares one shape — sequence. The narrow answer shipped in days and taught us more than the broad one would have.

03

Recognize opportunity

Rewards as a feature was unconventional but deeply aligned with our vision for engaging learning. Although pre-build demos were promising, we were able to take the risk with a safety net because it was client-funded.
